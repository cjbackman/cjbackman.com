- The made-up war between product and engineering
  - You don’t have to have it
  - Comes from a belief that “product is working for the business (hidden agenda) and doesn’t understand technology” and “engineers just wants to tinker and play around with the tech”
  - Both of those views look down on the other
  - What incentives are in place if pushing for features only can be a successful approach? Output rather outcome
  - Good exercise for engineers to argue for their improvements based on the value
  - Lack of direction, alignment & strategy can create this dissonance
  - Healthy debate in the teams yes, that’s why you have an empowered team with the product troika
  - But no need to artificially create it on a larger scale, across the organization
  - If you have friction between product and engineering, in the absence of strategy, someone is creating it.
  - Figure out why it’s happening and focus on gelling the product teams back together again.
  - Instead of creating artificial structures that “ensure a healthy balance” between product and engineering
  - If you have strong leadership and empowered teams, that healthy balance will be baked into the teams and they are rewarded for value, not features
  - Need to gel your team
  - Link to tech debt
  - False dichotomy
  - Healthy tension - where does the idea come from? Democracy, debates? Is that perspective wrong? If yes, why? Confused? Should exist during alignment but not as a constant fight.
  - The way to align - trust and value. It’s usually a failure to communicate value that leads to misunderstanding and low trust. The low trust exacerbates the next discussion. People are already convinced the incentives are different. They are not. At least they shouldn’t. Both should care about value.
  - More solutions: mentoring & coaching (discuss the topic, understand motivation and potential distrust)
  - Role modeling: call out, and explain over and over again why the rift is not real
  - This happens in organizations where the role of product management is misunderstood (by engineering, product, and business). Where you have product owners and feature factories.
- There must be an appreciation of each other's craft. Have engineers read Cagan and Cutler. Have product people read Will Larson and Tanya (staff book author).
  As an engineering leader, you must invest in your relationship with your product counterpart, evangelize the value of a product to the engineering org and address any hypersensitivity.
